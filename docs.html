Dairy Sense

A Sustainable Smart Dairy Farm, built twice: once as a 3 × 2 ft tabletop model, and once as a living 3D digital twin that runs the whole farm in your browser. This page explains every part of both, how to operate them, how to wire the electronics, and how to present the project.

  • Presented by Siddhi Keshari, Vaibhav Laxmi Pandey, Sakshi Devi, Tanu Yadav, Arya Gupta
  • College United University, Prayagraj

Overview

A normal dairy farm buys electricity, pumps fresh water for every wash, throws dung on a heap and notices a sick animal only when she stops eating. Dairy Sense shows a farm where each of those problems is solved by connecting it to another part of the farm. The sun runs the pump. The wash water is filtered and used again. Sensors watch the shed and every animal. The dung becomes cooking gas, backup electricity, compost and, in the end, the fodder the animals eat.

The project has two halves that tell the same story:

The physical model

A 3 × 2 ft board with a cardboard shed, a mini solar panel and battery, two water tanks with a filter and pump, a DHT11 temperature and humidity sensor, an ESP8266 Wi-Fi board, an L293D motor driver, an LCD and an IR sensor at the gate.

The digital twin

The same board in 3D, with five animals living a full day every two minutes. A farm engine works out the energy, water, climate, herd behaviour, health-belt readings, GPS positions, biogas, compost and fodder, and a phone app on screen shows what the farmer would see.

Three things have no fixed time. The digester works all day and night. The biogas generator starts whenever the gas holder is nearly full or the battery is low. The cooling starts whenever the shed gets too hot.

Farm base and cow shed

Everything stands on one board, 3 ft wide and 2 ft deep. The shed is at the back left, with the paddock in front of it. The water system, the solar panel and the battery are along the back. The biogas plant and the fodder plot are at the front right.

Green grass sheet for the farm area, cardboard for the shed, and separate places for feeding and for drinking water. The purpose of this part is a clean, organised home for the animals.

The herd

Five animals live on the farm: three cows and two buffaloes. This table is read from the engine.

"Heat sensitivity" multiplies how strongly an animal reacts to heat. The two zebu breeds, Sahiwal and Gir, cope best (× 0.70). Gauri, the Holstein-Friesian cross, gives the most milk and suffers most in heat (× 1.15).

In the 3D scene every animal is the same cow picture, repainted in her own coat and standing as a cut-out card that turns to face you, the way printed animals stand on a real tabletop model. The two buffaloes are that picture in Murrah colours.

What an animal does all day

Nobody scripts the animals. Each one decides what to do next from a few simple needs:

  • Thirst rises all the time, faster when she is heat stressed and four times slower while she rests. Above 0.55 in the day (0.95 at night) she walks to the drinking trough, if one of its two places is free, and drinks up to 22 L.
  • Hunger rises too. Grazing brings it down. Above 0.6 she goes to the feed trough.
  • Otherwise she picks at random: graze (68 %), lie down and rest (22 %) or stand a while (10 %).
  • At milking time every animal walks to her own place at the feed trough. At night each goes to her own resting spot in the shed.
  • She drops a 2.2 kg dung pat every 2.3 to 3.9 hours, but never while lying down.

Animals can pass between the shed and the paddock only through the gate, which is how the IR gate sees every one of them.

Milking and washing

Milking runs from 06:00 to 07:00 and from 17:00 to 18:00. At the end of each milking the engine works out every animal's yield:

yield = usual yield × (1 − 0.30 × heat stress since last milking)
                    × (1 − 0.45 × sickness)
                    × a random factor between 0.96 and 1.04

So a hot day or a fever shows up in the milk can. If an animal gives less than 80 % of her usual yield, the app raises a "milk yield dropped" warning and writes it into her health record. After each milking the milk chiller runs for three hours and the shed is washed for one hour.

In the twin, go to stop 2 and press Skip to milking time, then Wash the shed. Watch the animals line up at the trough, then follow the blue beads from the shed drain to the collection tank.

Solar energy

The panel charges the battery in the day. The battery runs everything, day and night. When the battery is low, the biogas generator helps. Only if all three fail does the farm take power from the grid.

A mini solar panel, a battery and LED lights. The purpose is to show that the farm can cut its dependence on conventional electricity.

An 800 W panel and a 3 kWh battery. Lights switch on by themselves whenever the sun is nearly down.

How much the panel makes

The sun rises at 06:00, is highest at 12:00 and sets at 18:00. The engine describes the height of the sun as a number from 0 to 1:

sun   = sin(π × (hour − 6) / 12)        between 06:00 and 18:00, otherwise 0
solar = 800 W × sun^1.15 × weather      weather: sunny 1.00, cloudy 0.36, rain 0.14

It is "night" for the lights whenever sun is under 0.06, which is about a quarter of an hour either side of sunrise and sunset.

What uses the power

LoadPowerWhen it runs
Controller, sensors, Wi-Fi15 WAlways
LED lights120 WAt night in Auto. The phone app can force them On or Off.
Water pump370 WWhile water is being recycled
Fans and foggers160 WWhile the shed is too hot
Milk chiller250 WFor 3 hours after each milking
Dung rover30 WWhile it is driving

Every instant the engine adds up solar + generator − load and moves that much energy into or out of the battery. A full battery wastes the surplus. An empty battery makes the grid supply the shortfall, and that grid energy is what lowers the "renewable power" KPI.

The biogas generator

The generator makes 800 W and burns 0.55 m³ of gas per hour. It follows two rules:

  • Start when the gas holder is over 92 % full (so gas is never wasted), or when the battery is under 30 % and there is more than 0.5 m³ of gas.
  • Stop when the gas falls under 0.2 m³, or the battery is over 60 % and the holder is under 70 %, or the battery is over 99 %.

The app warns "Battery low" under 20 % and "Running on grid power" whenever the grid is in use.

At stop 3 press Skip to night and watch the lamps come on by themselves while the battery bar starts to fall. Then press Make it cloudy, set the speed to 12x and watch a few days: the generator chip in the phone's Farm tab switches on more often.

Water recycling

Washing a shed uses a lot of water, and most of it runs away. Here it is caught, cleaned and used again, and rain from the shed roof is added to it.

Two tanks, a filter column of gravel, sand and charcoal, and a small pump moved by the L293D driver. The four steps are collection, filtration, pumping and circulation.

When the pump runs

In Auto the pump follows two thresholds, far enough apart that it does not keep switching on and off:

  • On when the collection tank holds more than 110 L and the storage tank is under 93 % full.
  • Off when the collection tank falls under 25 L or the storage tank reaches 98 %.

In Manual the farmer switches it from the phone. It still protects itself: if the collection tank falls under 4 L, or the storage tank is full, the pump stops and the app says why ("Dry-run protection switched the pump off").

Where the water goes

UseRateRule
Shed wash150 L/hOne hour after each milking, or when you press Wash shed. 85 % drains back to the collection tank.
Cooling foggers25 L/hWhile the cooling is on and the storage tank holds more than 10 L. This water evaporates.
Fodder irrigation110 L/hStarts in the 06:30 to 09:30 or 16:00 to 18:30 window if soil moisture is under 45 %, it is not raining and the storage tank holds more than 80 L. Stops when moisture passes 92 %, the tank falls under 30 L, or it rains.
Rain harvest+260 L/hInto the collection tank whenever it rains, until the tank is full.
Fresh top-up+300 L/hOnly when the storage tank is under 110 L and the collection tank is under 110 L too. Stops at 330 L.
Drinking trough+200 L/hRefilled from the fresh line when under half full (60 L), until 97 %.

The "water reused" KPI is the share of all water used that came through the pump rather than from the fresh line.

Why not let the animals drink the recycled water? A gravel, sand and charcoal filter removes dirt, colour and smell. It does not remove germs. Filtered wash water is good enough to wash a floor or water a crop, and not good enough to drink.

At stop 4 press Wash the shed and open the phone's Farm tab. The collection bar rises, the pump button turns ON by itself, and the storage bar climbs back. Then press Make it rain.

Smart monitoring

A sensor in the shed measures temperature and humidity. A small controller reads it, shows it on a display, sends it to the farmer's phone over Wi-Fi, and switches the pump.

PartIts job
DHT11Measures air temperature and humidity in the shed. It can be read about once every two seconds.
ESP8266A small computer with Wi-Fi built in. It reads the sensors, decides, and talks to the phone. It creates its own hotspot, so no router is needed.
16 × 2 LCDShows the reading on the model itself: Temp:31.5°C on the first line, Hum:64% Pump:ON on the second.
L293DA motor driver. An ESP8266 pin can only give a tiny current. The L293D takes that small signal and switches the pump's own, stronger supply.

THI: how hot the animals feel

Cattle suffer from heat sooner in damp air, because sweat and breath cool them less. The Temperature-Humidity Index puts temperature and humidity into one number. The engine uses this formula, with T in °C and RH in percent:

THI = (1.8 × T + 32) − (0.55 − 0.0055 × RH) × (1.8 × T − 26)

What the farm does about it

  • Fans and foggers switch on at THI 79 and off again at THI 76.5. The decision uses the THI the shed would have without cooling, so that the cooling does not switch itself off the moment it starts to work.
  • While the cooling runs, the shed settles 4.5 °C cooler and 6 points more humid than it would be, the fans draw 160 W and the foggers use 25 L of stored water per hour.
  • If the shed THI still reaches 79 with the cooling on, the app raises the warning "Shed heat stress".
  • An animal starts to feel heat stress above THI 77. At THI 89 it is at its maximum. Stress raises her body temperature by up to 0.9 °C and her heart rate by up to 16 beats a minute, both multiplied by her heat sensitivity, and it lowers her next milk yield.
  • The paddock is hotter than the shed in sunshine: the engine adds up to 2.2 °C in full sun.

Two thresholds instead of one is called hysteresis. With a single threshold at 79, a shed hovering around 79 would switch the fans on and off every few seconds, which wears out motors and relays. The pump and the generator use the same trick.

The weather behind it

The outside air follows a daily wave: coolest at 03:00, warmest at 15:00.

air temperature = 23.5 + 4.5 × sin(2π × (hour − 9) / 24)     19 °C to 28 °C on a normal day
                  + 8.0  in a heat wave
                  − 1.2  when cloudy,  − 3.5  in rain
humidity        = 62 − 2 × (temperature − 23.5)
                  + 8 when cloudy,  + 26 in rain              kept between 22 % and 97 %

The shed is 1 °C warmer than the outside air before cooling. When the real ESP8266 is linked, its DHT11 reading replaces this model for the shed.

At stop 5 press Start a heat wave. On the kiosk screen and in the phone's Farm tab the THI climbs into "Heat stress", then the fans and foggers start. Press Switch the pump by hand to see the L293D obey the phone.

Animal detection gate

An infrared beam shines across the gate between the shed and the paddock. Each time an animal walks through, she breaks the beam. A red LED flashes and the controller counts.

One IR sensor module and one red LED. Most IR modules pull their output LOW when they see something. The firmware counts each new beam break once and lights the LED for half a second.

Every animal that walks through the gate flashes the LED and adds one to the crossings counter. The phone's Farm tab shows how many animals are in the shed and in the paddock, and the last crossing: who, which way, and when.

What the count is good for

  • Knowing where the herd is without walking the farm.
  • A log of every crossing with its time.
  • The same beam can warn of movement at night.
  • A check on the GPS: when an animal misses milking, the app says "the gate counter and GPS agree she never came in".

Be honest about this in a viva. A single IR beam can only tell that something passed. It cannot tell which animal it was, or which way she went. The twin shows names and directions because the simulation already knows where every animal is. To get direction on real hardware you would need two beams side by side and to see which breaks first. To know which animal, you need her belt.

At stop 6 press Call the herd in for milking and count the red flashes as the five animals walk in.

AI health belt

Every animal wears a belt with five sensors. The belt does not just report numbers. It learns what is normal for that one animal and tells the farmer when she stops being normal, usually before a person would notice.

The belt exists only in the twin. There is no belt hardware in this project, and its readings come from a model of an animal's body, not from a real cow.

The five sensors

SensorMeasuresWhat it reveals
TemperatureBody temperature, °CFever, heat stress
Heart rateBeats per minuteStress, illness, excitement
AccelerometerAn activity index: about 4 lying down, 35 grazing, 75 walkingRestlessness (estrus), dullness (illness)
RuminationMinutes of cud-chewing per hour: about 40 lying down, 6 grazingDigestion. A sick cow stops chewing the cud.
GPSPosition, walking speed, distance walkedLameness, isolation from the herd, straying. See GPS tracking.

How the twin makes the readings

The engine keeps three hidden numbers for each animal, each from 0 to 1: heat stress, sickness and lameness. The belt readings are worked out from them and from what she is doing:

body temperature = her normal temperature
                 + 0.15 × sin(2π × (hour − 10) / 24)     a small daily rhythm
                 + 0.9 × heat stress + 1.7 × sickness     plus sensor noise of ± 0.04 °C

heart rate       = her resting rate + 0.16 × activity
                 + 16 × heat stress + 14 × sickness + 6 if in estrus     noise ± 1.5

activity         → value for what she is doing × (1 − 0.55 × sickness) × (1 − 0.25 × heat stress) × 1.9 if in estrus
rumination       → value for what she is doing × (1 − 0.60 × sickness) × (1 − 0.40 × heat stress) × 0.6 if in estrus

A fever builds up over about an hour and fades over about two hours once treated. Estrus lasts 14 hours. A lame animal walks at as little as 40 % of her normal speed and lies down more.

Learning each animal's baseline

A buffalo's normal temperature is lower than a cow's. One animal is naturally livelier than another. A single limit for the whole herd would either miss a quiet animal's fever or keep raising false alarms for a lively one. So the belt compares each animal only with herself.

Before you see anything, the twin silently runs one whole farm day. From that day it stores, for every animal:

  • her average body temperature,
  • her average activity and rumination, separately for day (06:30 to 21:30) and night,
  • her busiest normal two hours of activity, which is the ceiling for "restless",
  • her normal walking speed from GPS.

How the AI scores a reading

Every 15 farm minutes the belt stores a sample, and the AI works out four scores from 0 to 1. The code is the function evaluate() in src/sim.js. First it prepares four comparisons:

NameMeaningHow it is worked out
herdHeatHow hot the shed is, 0 to 1(shed THI − 77) ÷ 12, kept between 0 and 1
dTTemperature above her own baseline, with the effect of hot weather taken outher average over the last hour − her baseline − 0.8 × herdHeat
actRIs she as active as usual?her average activity over 3 hours ÷ her baseline for this time of day
rumRIs she chewing the cud as usual?her average rumination over 6 hours ÷ her baseline for this time of day
actXIs she more active than she ever normally is?her average activity over 2 hours ÷ her busiest normal 2 hours
mobRIs she walking at her normal speed?her GPS walking speed over the last 2 hours ÷ her normal speed

In the formulas below, ramp(x) means "x, but never less than 0 or more than 1".

fever    = 0.7 × ramp((dT − 0.6) / 0.8)
         + 0.2 × ramp((0.85 − rumR) / 0.4) × ramp(dT / 0.5)
         + 0.1 × ramp((0.85 − actR) / 0.4) × ramp(dT / 0.5)

heat     = herdHeat × ramp((temperature over the last hour − baseline) / 0.5)

estrus   = ramp((actX − 1.12) / 0.4)            only if dT is under 0.6, otherwise 0

lameness = ramp((0.74 − mobR) / 0.22)           only if dT is under 0.5, estrus is under 0.3
                                                and there was no fever verdict in the last 3 hours

In words:

  • Fever is mostly temperature: the score starts when she is 0.6 °C above her own normal and is strongest at 1.4 °C. Less cud-chewing and less activity add to it, but only if she is also warm, so a quiet hour on its own is never read as illness.
  • Heat stress needs two things at once: a hot shed, and her own temperature up.
  • Estrus ("in heat", ready to breed) is restlessness without fever: activity more than 12 % above her busiest normal two hours.
  • Lameness is slow walking without fever: the score starts when her GPS walking speed falls under 74 % of normal.

Then the verdict:

  1. The highest of the four scores wins. If it is under 0.5, she is healthy. On a tie the order is fever, heat stress, estrus, lameness.
  2. A verdict must repeat on two readings in a row, 30 farm minutes, before the alert is raised. Two healthy readings in a row clear it. One odd reading never rings the farmer's phone.
  3. A fever alert is critical. The other three are warnings.

The health score shown in her record is:

health = 100 − 48 × fever − 26 × heat − 6 × estrus − 30 × lameness
             − 10 × (how far her thirst is past its limit) − 12 if she is outside the geofence

Her record also shows a five-part health breakdown, each part from 0 to 100: temperature, rumination, activity, mobility (from GPS) and hydration (hours since her last trough visit: full marks up to 14 hours, zero at 26).

How does it tell a fever from a hot day? On a hot day every animal's temperature rises together, and the shed THI rises too. The AI subtracts 0.8 °C × herdHeat from each animal's rise before judging fever. A fever is one animal hot on her own. Heat stress is the whole herd warm together with a hot shed.

Is it really AI? It is a small, explainable expert system: per-animal learned baselines plus scoring rules whose every weight you can read above. It is not a neural network, and it was not trained on data from real animals. The advantage is that it can always say why it raised an alert, and the app shows that reason to the farmer.

Run the experiment

This figure starts a fresh farm, lets it run normally from 08:00 to 10:00, then secretly makes one animal ill and steps the engine forward 15 farm minutes at a time. Nothing tells the AI what was done. The numbered markers show when it noticed.

Run all three conditions for the same animal and compare. Fever: temperature climbs, activity and rumination sink. Estrus: temperature stays flat while activity runs above her busiest normal. Lameness: temperature stays flat and only the GPS walking speed gives her away. Fever is found fastest, usually in under two farm hours. Estrus and lameness usually take one and a half to four. GPS tracking and virtual fence

Each belt also reports where the animal is. That one extra reading gives the farmer four things: a live map, an alarm when an animal leaves the farm, a fence that needs no posts or wire, and two more early health signs.

Like the rest of the belt, GPS is simulated. For the map, the 36 × 24 inch board stands for a 72 × 48 m farm near Prayagraj: one inch on the board is 2 m on the map.

The GPS fix

  • Every belt sends a new position every 3 farm minutes.
  • A real GPS is less accurate under a roof, and the model copies that: in the open it reports an accuracy of 1.2 to 2 m with 9 to 12 satellites, and in the shed 4.5 to 6.5 m with 4 to 6 satellites.
  • Every 6 farm minutes a breadcrumb is added to her trail. The last 60 are kept.
  • The belt adds up the distance she has walked today, and how many hours she has spent grazing, resting, walking and feeding.

Geofence: nobody gets lost

The shed and the paddock together are the safe zone. If a belt reports a position outside it, the app raises a critical alert at once: "Name has left the safe zone". The farmer presses Guide back, the belt plays an audio cue, and the animal turns round and walks home. While she is out she is not milked, and her health score loses 12 points.

At stop 8 press Leave the side gate open. The phone switches to the Map tab and one animal wanders out toward the fodder plot. When the alert arrives, press Guide her back.

Virtual fence: resting half the pasture

Grass that is grazed every day never recovers. The usual answer is to divide the pasture with fences and move the herd between the parts, which costs wire, posts and labour. Here the fence is only a line on the map. When an animal reaches it, her belt gives an audio cue and she turns back.

  • The paddock is split down the middle into a west cell and an east cell. The strip in front of the gate stays open so the animals can always reach the shed.
  • Grazing eats a cell down. Thin grass feeds an animal less: at full cover grazing satisfies her at 110 % of the base rate, on bare ground at 55 %.
  • A rested cell regrows in daylight, faster in rain and more slowly as it approaches full cover. A cell that is being grazed regrows at less than half that speed.
  • With Auto on, the herd is moved when the grazed cell falls under 35 % cover and the other cell is at least 25 points better.
  • The farmer can also choose West, East or Whole by hand. "Whole" removes the fence, so neither cell rests.

Biogas, compost and fodder

Dung is the one thing a dairy farm never runs out of. On this farm none of it leaves. It is turned into cooking gas, electricity and compost, and the compost grows the next meal.

The purpose of this part of the project is to show that waste is a resource: dung collected separately, a biogas plant, and compost for the soil.

1. The dung rover

A small rover waits at its dock at the edge of the paddock. It sets off when two or more dung pats are waiting, or when one has been lying for more than an hour. It drives to the nearest pat, picks it up, and keeps collecting until it carries 11 kg or nothing is left. Then it returns and tips its load into the digester inlet. If ten pats pile up, the app warns "Dung build-up".

2. The digester

The digester is a sealed tank where bacteria break dung down without air and give off biogas, which is mostly methane. The plant on the board is a floating-drum type: the gas collects under an upturned drum, and the drum rises as it fills. In the twin you can watch the green drum go up and down.

StepRule in the engine
FeedingDung moves from the inlet into the digester at up to 7 kg per hour.
DigestionEach hour, one sixteenth of the material in the digester is digested.
GasEach kilogram digested gives 0.042 m³ of biogas. The holder is full at 3 m³.
SlurryEach kilogram digested leaves 0.28 kg of compost.

3. Using the gas

  • The stove burns 0.4 m³ per hour at meal times: 07:00 to 08:12, 12:24 to 13:24 and 19:00 to 20:12, as long as the holder has more than 0.15 m³. The twin counts every cubic metre burned as 0.43 kg of LPG that did not have to be bought.
  • The generator turns gas into 800 W of electricity when the holder is nearly full or the battery is low. Its rules are in Solar energy.

4. Compost and fodder

  • At 08:30 each morning, if at least 12 kg of compost is ready, it is spread on the fodder plot. Each kilogram raises the soil fertility a little, and fertility slowly wears off again.
  • The crop grows only in daylight. It grows faster with more sun, wetter soil and richer soil:
growth per hour = 0.04 × sun × weather × (0.3 + 0.7 × soil moisture) × (0.55 + 0.9 × soil fertility)
                  weather counts as at least 0.45, so the crop still grows on dull days
  • When the crop is fully grown it is harvested: 110 kg of green fodder goes to the feed store, and the plot starts again from 18 %. In sunny weather that happens every four or five farm days.
  • Three times a day the feed trough is filled from the store. Whatever the store cannot supply is counted as bought feed.

That closes the loop: fodder → herd → dung → digester → compost → fodder.

At stop 9 press Fast-forward one day and watch the rover, the drum and the crop. Then open the Farm tab: "Biogas and soil" shows the gas holder, the crop, the dung waiting and the compost ready.

Hardware: build and flash

The physical model has one electronic node. It reads the shed climate, shows it on the LCD, drives the pump, watches the gate, and serves all of it over Wi-Fi so the twin can show and control the real model.

The firmware sketch was written to match the twin's API, but its author has not run it on hardware. Treat the wiring below as a careful starting point. Check every pin against your own board before you power the pump.

Bill of materials

NodeMCU or Wemos D1 mini (ESP8266)1The controller and the Wi-Fi hotspot
DHT11 sensor1Temperature and humidity, powered from 3V3

The rest of the model is not electronic: the 3 × 2 ft board, grass sheet, cardboard shed, the mini solar panel with its battery and LED lights, the two tanks and the gravel, sand and charcoal filter.

Wiring

The same wiring as a table, copied from the header of the sketch:

DHT11  DATA -> D4      (VCC 3V3, GND)
LCD    SDA  -> D2, SCL -> D1   (VCC 5V/VIN, GND)
L293D  EN1  -> D5, IN1 -> D6, IN2 -> D7
       OUT1/OUT2 -> pump motor, VCC2 -> pump supply (5-9 V), GNDs joined
IR     OUT  -> D0      (most modules pull LOW when they see something)
LED    red  -> D8 through a 220 ohm resistor to GND

Two things the sketch header does not spell out. The L293D chip also needs its own logic supply (the pin usually marked VCC1 or VSS) connected to 5 V. And if your IR module gives HIGH when it sees something, change IR_ACTIVE_LOW to false near the top of the sketch.

Flashing the board

  1. Install the Arduino IDE and add ESP8266 board support through the Boards Manager.
  2. In the Library Manager install DHT sensor library by Adafruit together with Adafruit Unified Sensor, and LiquidCrystal I2C by Frank de Brabander.
  3. Open firmware/dairy_sense_esp8266/dairy_sense_esp8266.ino.
  4. Choose your board (NodeMCU or Wemos D1 mini) and its port, then upload.
  5. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. You should see Hotspot up, open http://192.168.4.1. The LCD shows "Dairy Sense / starting..." and then the readings.

From then on:

  • The real DHT11 drives the twin's shed climate. Warm the sensor with your hand or your breath and the twin's THI rises. Past 79, its fans and foggers start.
  • The twin's pump decision drives the real pump. Whenever the pump in the twin switches, in Auto or by the phone's button, the twin sends the command and the L293D switches the real motor.
  • A real beam break flashes the twin's gate LED. The device keeps its own crossings count. The twin's count still follows its simulated animals.

The twin asks the device for its state every 2 seconds. If three requests in a row fail, it quietly goes back to simulated sensors and keeps retrying. Disconnect does the same on purpose.

Open the twin by double-clicking index.html, as usual. If you ever put the pages on a website served over https, browsers will refuse to call a plain http device, and the link will not work.

Device API

The ESP8266 answers three plain HTTP addresses. Every answer is JSON, and every answer carries headers that let a web page on another address read it (Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *), plus Cache-Control: no-store.

GET /api/state

Returns everything the device knows.

{"temp":31.5,"hum":64,"pump":false,"ir":false,"crossings":12,"uptime":345}
FieldMeaning
tempShed temperature in °C, one decimal. null until the DHT11 has given a first good reading.
humRelative humidity in percent, whole number. null until the first good reading.
pumptrue while the pump is running.
irtrue if the beam has been broken since the last time /api/state was read. Reading it resets it, so a short crossing between two polls is not missed.
crossingsBeam breaks counted since the board was switched on.
uptimeSeconds since the board was switched on.

GET /api/pump?on=1

Switches the pump: on=1 for on, on=0 for off. It answers with the same JSON as /api/state, showing the new pump state. Without the on parameter it changes nothing and just reports.

GET http://192.168.4.1/api/pump?on=1

{"temp":31.5,"hum":64,"pump":true,"ir":false,"crossings":12,"uptime":351}

As a safety measure the firmware switches the pump off by itself if the sensor ever reports more than 60 °C, which can only mean the reading is nonsense.

Everything else

  • GET / returns the small human-readable status page described in The hotspot.
  • Any other address returns status 404 with {"error":"not found"}.

You can try the API from any browser or from a terminal on a computer that has joined the hotspot:

curl http://192.168.4.1/api/state
curl "http://192.168.4.1/api/pump?on=1"

Viva questions

Likely questions from judges, with short answers that match what the project really does.

1. What is a digital twin?

A computer model of a real system that behaves like it. Ours has the same parts as the tabletop model, follows the same rules, and can be linked to the real sensor so that the two move together.

2. Why does a dairy farm need to be "sustainable"?

A dairy uses a lot of water and electricity and produces a lot of dung. If the water is reused, the power comes from the sun, and the dung becomes gas and compost, the farm costs less to run and pollutes less.

3. What is THI and why do you use it instead of temperature?

The Temperature-Humidity Index. Cattle cool themselves by evaporation, which works badly in damp air. THI combines both: (1.8T + 32) − (0.55 − 0.0055 RH)(1.8T − 26). Below 72 is comfortable, 72 to 79 mild stress, 79 to 89 heat stress, 89 and above danger.

4. Why do the fans start at 79 but stop at 76.5?

That gap is hysteresis. With one threshold the fans would switch on and off every few seconds when the shed sits near it. Two thresholds make it switch once and stay.

5. What does the L293D do? Why not connect the pump straight to the ESP8266?

An ESP8266 pin gives only a few milliamps at 3.3 V. A pump motor needs far more, at 5 to 9 V. The L293D takes the small control signal and switches the pump's own supply.

6. Why does the ESP8266 make its own hotspot?

So the project works anywhere, with no router and no internet. A phone or laptop joins the network "DairySense" and opens 192.168.4.1.

7. Can the animals drink the recycled water?

No. Gravel, sand and charcoal remove dirt, colour and smell, but not germs. Recycled water is used for washing, cooling and irrigation. Drinking water comes from the fresh line.

8. How does the pump know when to run?

It starts when the collection tank has more than 110 L and the storage tank is under 93 %. It stops when the collection tank is under 25 L or the storage tank is 98 % full. In manual mode it still stops itself if it would run dry.

9. What happens on a cloudy or rainy day?

Solar output falls to 36 % when cloudy and 14 % in rain. The battery covers the gap. If it drops under 30 %, the biogas generator starts. The grid is the last resort.

10. How does the IR sensor know which animal passed, and which way?

It does not. One beam only knows that something passed, so the real model counts crossings. The twin can show names and directions because the simulation knows where each animal is. Direction on real hardware would need two beams. Identity needs the belt.

11. Is the health belt real?

No. It is simulated in the twin. Belts with these sensors do exist for real cattle. Ours shows the idea and the decision logic.

12. Is it really artificial intelligence?

It is an explainable expert system. It learns a baseline for each animal from a day of her own data, then scores each new reading against it with fixed rules. It is not a trained neural network. Its advantage is that it can state its reason.

13. Why a baseline for each animal instead of one limit for all?

Animals differ. Our buffaloes run at 38.2 °C and our Holstein cross at 38.6 °C. One fixed limit would miss a fever in one and raise false alarms for the other.

14. How does it tell a fever from a hot day?

On a hot day the whole herd warms up and the shed THI is high, so the AI subtracts the expected heat effect before judging fever. A fever is one animal hot on her own, usually chewing the cud less.

15. What is rumination and why measure it?

Chewing the cud: bringing food back up to chew it again. A healthy cow does it for hours a day, mostly lying down. A drop is one of the earliest signs of illness.

16. How does detecting estrus help the farmer?

A cow can be bred only in a short window. A cow in heat is restless: her activity rises well above her normal. The belt spots that and tells the farmer the best breeding window is the next 12 to 18 hours.

17. What is a geofence, and what is a virtual fence?

A geofence is a boundary on the GPS map. If a belt reports a position outside it, the farmer is alerted. A virtual fence uses the same idea inside the pasture: the belt gives an audio cue at an invisible line, so the herd grazes one half while the other half regrows, with no wire or posts.

18. How much biogas does the farm make?

In the model, each kilogram of dung digested gives 0.042 m³ of gas, and the recorded sunny day at the top of this page produces close to 3 m³. These are model values chosen so the loop is visible, not measurements.

19. How is the farm score calculated?

From five percentages: water reuse (25 %, counted double), renewable power (25 %), waste collected (20 %), animal welfare (20 %) and hygiene (10 %).

20. Are the numbers real?

They are illustrative. The rules and their thresholds are sensible, but the quantities were chosen for a clear demonstration, and time is compressed. The only real measurement is the DHT11 when the hardware is linked.

21. What would you improve next?

Test the firmware on real hardware. Add a second IR beam for direction, and level sensors in the real tanks so the pump runs from real water levels. Build one real belt with a temperature sensor and an accelerometer, and compare its data with the model.

One tick of the simulation

Time moves in small steps called ticks, each at most 0.04 farm hours (2.4 farm minutes). At 1x speed, one second of real time is 0.2 farm hours, so about five ticks. Every tick does the same things in the same order:

  1. Clock and sun. Advance the time. At midnight reset the day's counters. At 09:00 check the herd register.
  2. Climate. Outside air, shed air (or the real DHT11 if linked), the cooling switch, THI.
  3. Daily routine. The milking flag, milk yields at 07:00 and 18:00, feed trough refills.
  4. Biogas plant. Feed the digester, digest, fill the holder, burn at the stove, decide the generator.
  5. Fodder plot. Spread compost, dry the soil, decide irrigation, grow the crop, harvest.
  6. Water. Rain, washing, fogging, irrigation, the pump, the fresh top-up, the drinking trough.
  7. Herd. Each animal: needs, dung, movement, the gate, GPS odometer and geofence, then her belt readings.
  8. Pasture and GPS. Regrow the grass, rotate the cells, take a GPS fix from every belt.
  9. Rover. Collect and deliver dung.
  10. Energy. Lights, chiller, solar, generator, total load, battery, grid.
  11. Belt AI. Every 15 farm minutes, evaluate() each animal.
  12. Alerts and KPIs. Farm-level alerts, the five KPIs and the score.

The engine uses its own seeded random numbers, so a fresh engine always behaves the same way. That is what makes the tests, and the figures on this page, repeatable.

With the twin open, press F12 and type in the console. The engine is at dairySense.sim. Try dairySense.sim.inject('DS-03', 'fever'), dairySense.sim.energy, or dairySense.sim.advance(24) to skip a day at once.

Adding a sensor

Say you want a real water-level sensor on the storage tank.

  1. Firmware. Read the sensor in loop() and add a field to stateJson(), for example "level":72. Add the same field to firmware/mock-device.mjs so you can test without hardware.
  2. Link. In src/live.js, copy the new field into sim.live inside poll(), the way temp and hum are copied.
  3. Engine. In tick() in src/sim.js, use it when it is present, the way the climate block uses s.live.temp. Decide what the farm should do with it, and raise an alert with condition() if needed.
  4. Interface. Show it in refreshFarm() in src/ui.js.
  5. Recording. To have it in Cow Flow and in the charts here, add it to the row in recordDay() and to the NUM list in src/trace.js.
  6. Test. Add a check to test/sim.test.mjs, then run npm test and npm run build.

Adding an animal

  1. Add a line to the HERD list at the top of src/sim.js: tag, name, kind, breed, coat, normal temperature, resting heart rate, litres per milking and heat sensitivity.
  2. Add a matching line to the RECORDS list just below it. The two lists are paired by position.
  3. Use a coat key that src/coats.js knows: holstein, sahiwal, gir, murrah or murrah2. An unknown key falls back to the Holstein coat.
  4. Run npm test, then npm run build.

The board is drawn for five animals. The feed trough has room for five places, the shed for five resting spots, and the GPS map has five track colours in src/tracking.js. A sixth animal will run in the engine, but she will stand past the end of the trough and outside the shed wall at night until feedSlot() and restSpot() in src/layout.js and the colour list are widened. Replacing one of the five is the easy change.

Glossary

API
A fixed set of addresses a program answers, so that other programs can talk to it. Here: /api/state and /api/pump.
Baseline
What is normal for one particular animal, learned from her own data.
Biogas
Gas made when bacteria break down dung without air. Mostly methane. It burns like LPG.
Compost
Rotted organic matter that feeds the soil. Here it is made from digester slurry.
DHT11
A cheap sensor that measures air temperature and humidity.
Digester
The sealed tank where dung turns into biogas. Ours is the floating-drum type: the gas lifts a drum.
Digital twin
A computer model that behaves like a real system and can be linked to it.
ESP8266
A small, cheap microcontroller with Wi-Fi built in. NodeMCU and Wemos D1 mini are boards that carry one.
Estrus
"In heat": the short period when a cow can be bred. Not the same as heat stress.
Farm hour
One hour of simulated time. It takes 5 real seconds at 1x speed.
Fodder
Green crop grown to feed cattle.
Geofence
A boundary on a GPS map that raises an alert when crossed.
Hotspot
A Wi-Fi network created by a device itself. The ESP8266 makes one named DairySense.
Hysteresis
Using one threshold to switch on and a different one to switch off, so a device does not chatter.
I2C
A two-wire connection (SDA and SCL) used by the LCD.
IR sensor
An infrared light and detector. When something blocks or reflects the beam, its output changes.
JSON
A simple text format for data, like {"temp":31.5}.
KPI
Key performance indicator: one number that sums up how well something is going.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour, a unit of energy. A 1000 W load running for one hour uses 1 kWh.
L293D
A motor driver chip. It lets a small control signal switch a motor's larger supply.
Lameness
Pain in a foot or leg that makes an animal walk slowly or unevenly.
LPG
Liquefied petroleum gas: the bottled cooking gas that biogas replaces.
Rumination
Chewing the cud. A healthy cow does it for hours a day.
Slurry
The wet material left in the digester after the gas has come off.
THI
Temperature-Humidity Index: one number for how hot the air feels to cattle.
Tick
One small step of the simulation, at most 2.4 farm minutes.
Virtual fence
A fence that exists only on the map. The belt cues the animal to turn back.
HF cross, Sahiwal, Gir, Murrah
The breeds in the herd. HF is Holstein-Friesian, a high-yielding European dairy breed. Sahiwal and Gir are Indian zebu breeds that stand heat well. Murrah is a dairy buffalo.

Limits and honesty

A model is useful only if you know where it stops being true. These are the limits of this one.

Time is compressed

  • One farm hour takes 5 seconds at 1x. A day takes two minutes.
  • The digester and the fodder crop are speeded up much more than that. The model's digester works through its contents in hours and its crop is ready to cut every four or five farm days, which is about ten minutes at 1x, so that the loop can be seen in a short demonstration. A real digester needs weeks, and a real fodder crop needs weeks to months.
  • The animals walk slowly for the size of the farm, so that you can follow them with your eyes.

The numbers are illustrative

  • Panel size, battery size, tank sizes, flow rates, gas yields and milk yields are model values chosen to make each loop visible and to keep the farm in balance. They are not measurements from a farm, and they should not be used to design one.
  • The THI formula and its bands are standard. The thresholds for switching the cooling, the pump and the generator are sensible choices, not standards.
  • The farm score is our own formula. It is a way to compare one day with another, not an official rating.

The belt AI

  • It is a set of explainable rules tuned on simulated animals. It has never seen data from a real cow.
  • Fever detection is quick and steady: between one and a quarter and one and three quarter farm hours in every run we tried.
  • Lameness depends on the animal walking, so it takes longer and varies: roughly one and a half to four farm hours in our runs.
  • Estrus is the slowest to show. It needs about two hours of clearly raised activity. In the twin an animal in heat paces the paddock, and across 80 test runs (all five animals, four start times, four random seeds) she was caught after 1¼ to 4¾ farm hours, with no false alarms in 36 ordinary farm days. A real cow can be quieter than that, which is why real systems also use mounting sensors or milk tests.
  • The thermal view is a drawing of belt temperatures, not a thermal camera.

The hardware

  • The firmware has not been tested on hardware by its author. See Hardware.
  • One IR beam counts crossings. It cannot tell direction or identity. See Animal detection gate.
  • When the real device is linked, only the shed temperature and humidity, the gate flash and the pump are shared. The tank levels in the twin are still simulated, so the twin may switch the real pump when the real tank is empty. Keep an eye on the real pump.
  • The hotspot password is printed in the sketch and on this page. It is fine for an exhibition and not for anything that matters.

This page

  • The charts, calculators and comparison table are computed by the farm engine in your browser, so they always match the twin you have. The formulas and thresholds written in the text were copied from the code by hand. If someone changes the engine, the text must be checked again.
